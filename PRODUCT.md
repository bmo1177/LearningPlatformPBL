# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Computer science students and self-learners studying Information Retrieval (IR). They range from undergraduates taking formal courses to independent developers wanting to understand search engines, indexing, and retrieval systems. They are comfortable with code but may be new to IR concepts. They use the platform during study sessions, often for 1-2 hours at a time, working through step-by-step lessons with code examples.

## Product Purpose

Teach Information Retrieval through hands-on, reflective learning. The platform guides learners through structured curriculum steps, provides code templates, quizzes for knowledge testing, reflection prompts, and an AI tutor for Socratic guidance. Success means learners can build working IR systems and articulate the concepts behind them.

## Brand Personality

Warm and encouraging. The platform should feel like a knowledgeable friend who believes the learner can do this. Voice is supportive without being patronizing. Tone is clear, direct, and optimistic. Emotional goal: confidence through guided practice.

## Anti-references

- Boring enterprise LMS (Canvas, Blackboard, Moodle) - generic, soul-crushing layouts
- Flashy but hollow SaaS landing pages - gradients, glassmorphism, empty hero sections
- Overly minimal designs that strip away context and feel cold/empty
- Cluttered old-school courseware with dense text walls and no visual hierarchy

## Design Principles

1. **Guided confidence**: Every design choice should make the learner feel capable, not overwhelmed. Progressive disclosure, clear next steps, visible progress.
2. **Show the work**: Code and content are the heroes. Design should frame and elevate learning material, not compete with it.
3. **Warmth through craft**: Encouragement comes from thoughtful design details, not decorative elements or emoji.
4. **Respect the learner's time**: Information hierarchy is critical. Scannable, not scrolly-for-forever. Every pixel earns its place.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance with enhanced focus states and contrast ratios. Reduced motion support via prefers-reduced-motion. Keyboard navigable sidebar and main content. Screen reader friendly step structure. Color is never the sole indicator of state.
